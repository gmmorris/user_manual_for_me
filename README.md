# A User Manual for Gidi Bravo-Rose (formerly Morris)

## Conditions I like to work in

I work most effectively with some form of white noise. Buzzing, well-lit offices and coffee shops suit me well, while quiet, dark rooms actually make it harder for me to slip into deep work. This is one of those coping mechanisms.

For this reason, I often work from our London co-working space or cafés.

## The hours I like to work

I'm a morning person and aim to start work between 8:00 and 9:00, after the nursery run, whether from home or my preferred location outside of home. I usually call it a day by 17:00-17:30, again because of the nursery run.

I'm often available in the evenings once the baby is down, and happy to help if you're blocked, but please don't feel you need to be working then.

## The best ways to communicate with me

Slack is the quickest way to get a response from me. Please don't hesitate to reach out at any time: I'll put my phone on silent if I'm busy, and I'm always happy to help a colleague out of hours. The idea of you being blocked on something I could help with bothers me far more than being asked for things out of hours.

I don't like answering my phone, as I'm often in spaces that are either too noisy for it or so quiet that my conversation would be forced on those around me. I do read texts, though, so SMS or WhatsApp are fine.

I stay on top of my email and my comments in Google Docs and Confluence daily. For anything non-urgent, expect a reply within a workday or so (if one is needed). Nudge me if I don't get back to you: I won't take offence, and I'll tell you if I need more time to respond properly.

I prefer to keep meeting time for bursts of collaboration, so I often ask people to use asynchronous communication as much as possible. That lets me digest information before the meeting and arrive ready with feedback and questions.

## The ways I like to receive feedback

Be direct. I'm much more comfortable with candour and difficult conversations than with things being left unspoken, so please just be straight with me.

I consider all feedback a learning opportunity and do my very best not to get defensive. You can [read my blog](https://gidi.io/team-dynamics/2018/08/04/feedback-and-cake/) to learn how I think about effective feedback, which should help you work out how best to talk with me about it.

Face to face is ideal. Failing that, a video call, and if that's hard, a phone call. I have a harder time taking feedback asynchronously such as instant messaging, so please use synchronous communication for it.

## I need to understand the why

Many people can start a task without knowing its purpose, because their brains let them skip the question. Mine won't. If I don't understand why we're doing something, I find it very hard to engage with it or follow it through. The upside is that I can't skip the why, so I always ask it, which helps make sure we're solving the right problem rather than a symptom.

So please over-communicate. Give me as much background as you can, and bring me into conversations early. I'd rather be in too many than too few.

## Some context on why I work this way

I'm neurodivergent: I have ADHD and dyslexia. They are a superpower for me, and also a liability at times.

A lot of how I like to work, as described above, is a direct reflection of the coping mechanisms I've developed over the years to be effective and happy while living with them. The white noise, the busy spaces I work in and my need for context are all part of that. Knowing the why should make me easier to work with, and I'm always happy to talk about any of it.

## Things I find hard

I'm an introvert and need time to recharge between conversations. I'm also a social creature, and I find it hard to get through a full day without some collaborative interaction with colleagues.

I really struggle with organising team social events, so please don't ask me to organise anything like that.

## What I believe

I dislike gatekeeping and intentional siloing. People do it to avoid conflict or discomfort, or because they put their own objectives ahead of the organisation’s. I find the second kind particularly frustrating.

Gatekeeping stops us improving. Letting things through, even when they’ll need attention afterwards, forces us to improve what the organisation can handle, and that makes the business more resilient. 

In hiring, that means looking at someone’s potential rather than what they lack: bring in a positive fit and help them fill the gaps. In how we work, it means trying new ideas and iterating rather than saying “that’s how we’ve always done it.”

## Things I love

I love collaborating, whether that's brainstorming, pair programming or random conversations about things.

I like hearing about other people's challenges. It helps me get comfortable with my own and gives me a chance to learn from your experience and ideas and share my own.

I love teaching and mentoring individuals outside the workplace, and I'm always open to taking on new mentees, time permitting.

## One more thing

I always bring my whole self to work, and I'm extremely uncomfortable when I can't.
